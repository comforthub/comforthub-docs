Every module below is mirrored between Zoho and Supabase. Almost all flow both ways. The interesting column is the last one: what happens here when someone deletes the record in Zoho.

| What a delete does | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **deactivate** | The row stays and is marked inactive. Nothing else is touched. |
| **delete** | The row is removed, along with only the child rows it owns. |
| **refuse and flag** | Nothing is written. A human-review flag is raised because this record moved money. The two systems now disagree until a person rules. |

Tested 2 September 2026 on a lead, a contact and a property: each was inactive on our side within 6 seconds of the delete in Zoho, and the records linked to it were not changed. A deactivated customer keeps its QuickBooks customer.

Tested again 4 September 2026 on one record in each of Contacts, Accounts, Leads, Properties and Assets: the row stayed, was marked inactive, and its link to the Zoho record was tombstoned, 42 to 110 seconds after the delete in Zoho. All but the last 0.1 to 0.3 seconds of that was Zoho holding the notification. Deleting the account did not touch its property or equipment.

Tested 4 September 2026 on a product, a stock material, a supplier and a purchase order, each made in Zoho for the test. Our side acted on every create, edit and delete within two seconds of Zoho sending word of it; Zoho itself took between 24 seconds and two minutes to send word of a create or an edit that day, and between 7 and 49 seconds for a delete. Every edit made on our side was in Zoho within five seconds. On delete, the product, the stock material and the supplier stayed and went inactive, the product's QuickBooks item went inactive with it, the supplier kept its branch, and the purchase order was removed.

One thing to know about purchase orders made in Zoho: our side gives the order its number, but Zoho only sees that number the next time the order is edited on our side. Until then the number field in Zoho is blank. Observed 4 September 2026, twice.

A purchase order made in Zoho on a supplier that has no branches yet gives that supplier a branch named **Default branch** on our side, and the branch stays when the order is deleted. Observed 4 September 2026.

Tested 4 September 2026 on a territory, a technician, a phone agent, a work order with two line items, and an appointment, each made in Zoho for the test. Every edit made in Zoho reached our side 25 seconds to 1½ minutes later, all of it Zoho holding the notice; every edit made on our side was in Zoho within 2 seconds. On delete, the territory, the technician and the phone agent stayed and went inactive, 55 seconds to 2½ minutes after the delete in Zoho. The appointment was removed 2¼ minutes after its delete, and the work order it pointed at kept both of its line items. The work order delete changed nothing on our side: 1¾ minutes after the delete in Zoho a human-review flag was raised, and the work order and both lines are still here while Zoho no longer has them. Closing that flag is [Resolve a human-review flag](/handbook/running/resolve-a-human-review-flag).

Two things to know about work orders, seen the same day: a line item changed on either side re-sends the whole work order with all its lines, in 2 to 3 seconds; and a work order made in Zoho gets a draft billing status from our side within 3 seconds of arriving, which Zoho shows as **Draft**.

A technician made in Zoho with an email address gets an app login and an invite email from our side within a second of arriving, and a phone agent made in Zoho has to name an existing app user or it cannot be created here. Seen 4 September 2026.

Tested 4 September 2026 on the four money modules, using the day's probe invoice INV008348, its two payments, and a subscription and a subscription payment typed into Zoho on the probe account. An invoice is never typed into Zoho; it is made here and was in Zoho 6 seconds later, and a payment recorded in QuickBooks was a Zoho Payment 3 to 4 seconds after it landed here. The subscription and the subscription payment typed into Zoho were here 8 and 4 seconds later. Every edit made on our side was in Zoho within 2 to 7 seconds; Zoho took 22 to 53 seconds to send word of an edit and 3 to 4 seconds for a delete, and our side acted within a second of the notice. Deleting the payment, the invoice and the subscription payment in Zoho changed nothing here: each raised a human-review flag 4 seconds after the delete, and each row still stands with its QuickBooks record while Zoho no longer has it. Deleting the subscription in Zoho left the row here, marked inactive, 3 seconds later.

Three things to know about the money modules, seen the same day:

- **A payment or a subscription edited in Zoho is silently undone.** Our side owns every mirrored field on those two (amount, method, plan, monthly amount, protection, active), so a value typed in Zoho is not applied here and is overwritten the next time our side pushes. Nothing flags it. The one field Zoho owns on a subscription payment, which subscription it belongs to, does reach us.
- **A subscription payment typed into Zoho looks paid here.** It gets a paid-on time and a made-up Stripe reference, and nothing tells Stripe or QuickBooks. The real path is Stripe; do not type one in.
- **The invoice status in Zoho did not take an edit.** Delivered, typed on the probe invoice, was back to Sent within half a minute; why is unverified. Zoho offers Created, Delivered and Void, our side accepts Draft, Sent and Void, so only Void means the same thing on both sides. Deleting an invoice in Zoho also unlinks its Zoho Payments, and Zoho sends no word of that.

Tested 4 September 2026 in the evening, after that day's three sync fixes went live, on a probe territory, stock material, purchase order and customer, a replayed phone call, a timesheet, a time-off request, a checklist submission and a booking confirmation email. Zoho was quick that evening: every create, edit and delete made in Zoho was here 3 to 8 seconds later, and every edit made on our side was in Zoho within 1 to 3 seconds.

- **A territory's arrival windows are Zoho's.** Two **Allowed Time Slots** ticked in Zoho were the two windows set here 4 seconds later; clearing one cleared it here in 6 seconds; the other three stayed off throughout. Our side never pushes the windows back. Until that day the ticks did not reach us at all (<Linear issue="COM-194" />).
- **The last-updated time moves.** On the stock material, the purchase order, the customer and the territory, an edit made in Zoho and an edit made on our side each moved the row's last-updated time: eight edits, eight moves. Until that day the time stayed at the moment the row first arrived, which could hide a changed row from the nightly sweep (<Linear issue="COM-191" />).
- **A call names its customer.** A call from a number that belongs to a customer had that customer in **Contact** on its Call History record in Zoho 1 second after the call ended. The customer is worked out here from the number; Zoho only reads it (<Linear issue="COM-169" />).
- **A timesheet, a time-off request and a checklist submission name their technician.** Each record pushed from our side had the right person in **Technician** in Zoho 1 to 3 seconds after being saved here. The app records the login; our side turns that into the technician. A technician whose Zoho record has been deleted cannot be named, so the test used a probe technician that still had one (<Linear issue="COM-172" />).
- **An email names the account.** The Email Tracking record for a booking confirmation showed the customer's **Account** in Zoho 1 second after the mail went out (<Linear issue="COM-170" />).

## Three rules that apply to every module

- **A locked record in Zoho is a one-way door.** A change made here is skipped and noted, and nothing alarms. Unlocking is the office's call.
- **A change never bounces.** When the sync writes a record, it marks the write as its own so the other side does not push it straight back. Seen again 4 September 2026 in five modules: each of our pushes came back from Zoho as one apply and no second push.
- **Zoho's notification is the slow part.** Our side applies a change in 0.1 to 2.4 seconds and pushes one in 1 to 2 seconds. Zoho took 19 to 128 seconds to send its notification on 4 September 2026. The one thing that stalls our side is a refused login refresh; see [The Supabase backend](/systems/supabase-backend).
