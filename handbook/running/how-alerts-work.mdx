Nothing in the engine waits for a person to notice a problem. A set of watches runs on timers, each looking for one kind of trouble, and each emails the operator when it finds some. The subject always starts with **[ops]** so the emails sort together.

## What is watched

- **Queues.** Work between systems travels as messages in queues. A message that fails all its retries is moved to a dead-letter box and reported. A queue that grows faster than it drains is reported.
- **Workers.** Every minute something nudges the two workers awake. A nudge that does not get through is reported.
- **Timed jobs.** A scheduled job that errors is reported.
- **Records that need a person.** Some outcomes are deliberately left for a person: a Zoho delete the rules refused, an invoice that could not be built, a payment that matched nothing. Each is flagged, and the count of open flags is reported.
- **Money.** A cancelled job whose invoice is still owed. An invoice whose amount differs between our side, QuickBooks and Zoho.
- **Housekeeping.** A property without its Street View image after a day. A payroll calendar with fewer than 60 days left. The nightly drift sweep not running.

The full list, with the subject each one uses, is on [Alerts](/reference/alerts). When each runs is on [Scheduled jobs](/reference/scheduled-jobs).

## Why you get one email, not fifty

Each watch remembers what it already reported. The same dead letter, the same open flag, the same stale sweep does not page again on the next run. You get a fresh email when the count changes or when the trouble clears and comes back. On 3 September 2026 two invoice refusals on one job produced one dead-letter email and one human-review email, fifteen minutes apart, and then silence.

## Where the emails go

To the operator's inbox, through the same guarded mailer as every other email. See [Email safety](/handbook/running/email-safety).
