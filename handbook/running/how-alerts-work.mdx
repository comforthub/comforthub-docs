Nothing in the engine waits for a person to notice a problem. A set of watches runs on timers, each looking for one kind of trouble, and each emails the operator when it finds some. The subject always starts with **[ops]** so the emails sort together.

## What is watched

- **Queues.** Work between systems travels as messages in queues. A message that fails all its retries is moved to a dead-letter box and reported. A queue that grows faster than it drains is reported.
- **Workers.** Every minute something nudges the two workers awake. A nudge that does not get through is reported. The nudge waits 5 seconds for an answer, except the one that wakes the alert watches themselves, which waits a minute: the watches take 3 to 14 seconds to run and send their mail, and until 4 September 2026 each of their own wake-ups was counted as a lost one.
- **Timed jobs.** A scheduled job that errors is reported.
- **Records that need a person.** Some outcomes are deliberately left for a person: a Zoho delete the rules refused, an invoice that could not be built, a payment that matched nothing, a record Zoho sent that can never apply as it stands. Each is flagged, and the count of open flags is reported. A refused Zoho login refresh raises one flag per spell, not one per retry, and the sync waits five minutes before asking Zoho again.
- **Money.** A cancelled job whose invoice is still owed. An invoice whose amount differs between our side, QuickBooks and Zoho; the finding is filed against QuickBooks and stays open until the amounts agree.
- **Housekeeping.** A property without its Street View image after a day. A payroll calendar with fewer than 60 days left. The nightly drift sweep not running.

The full list, with the subject each one uses, is on [Alerts](/reference/alerts). When each runs is on [Scheduled jobs](/reference/scheduled-jobs).

## Why you get one email, not fifty

Each watch remembers what it already reported. The same dead letter, the same open flag, the same stale sweep does not page again on the next run. You get a fresh email when the count changes or when the trouble clears and comes back. On 3 September 2026 two invoice refusals on one job produced one dead-letter email and one human-review email, fifteen minutes apart, and then silence.

The one exception that day was the wake-failure email, which went out three times in five minutes on 4 September 2026 because the alert watches' own wake-up, taking longer than 5 seconds, was being counted as a lost post on every run. Since the evening of 4 September that wake-up waits a minute. Watched 5 September at 00:05 UTC: a hand wake of the watches ran for 8 seconds and was recorded as answered, and the two cron wakes in the same second still waited 5 seconds and answered in time. One other post at midnight did time out at 5 seconds; it was 1 of 70 in that window and stayed under the five-in-fifteen-minutes line, so no email went out.

## Where the emails go

To the operator's inbox, through the same guarded mailer as every other email. See [Email safety](/handbook/running/email-safety).
