An alert names one kind of trouble and, where it can, the record involved. The job is to find the cause, fix it at the source, and watch the next run stay quiet.

## Before you start

- The alert email open.
- Access to the system that owns the record it names: Zoho, QuickBooks, or the engine's tables.

## Steps

<Steps>
  <Step title="Read the subject">
    The subject says what and how many: "holds 2 dead letters", "11 events await a person", "INV008343 is still collectible and its work order is cancelled". Match it to the row on [Alerts](/reference/alerts).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open the body">
    The body lists the records: the queue and message, the flagged events with their reasons, the invoice numbers. The reason is written in plain words, for example "work order WO-1189 has billable lines but 1 line with no QuickBooks item".
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fix the cause, not the symptom">
    A record problem is fixed in the system that owns the record (see [Who is the source of truth](/systems/who-is-the-source-of-truth)). A missing catalog item is added in Zoho. A cancelled job still owed is settled or voided in QuickBooks. A dead letter is put back on the queue only after its cause is gone; see [Redrive a dead letter](/handbook/running/redrive-a-dead-letter).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Close the flag">
    An event flagged for a person stays counted until it is marked reviewed; see [Resolve a human-review flag](/handbook/running/resolve-a-human-review-flag).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm it is quiet">
    The watch runs again on its schedule (most every 15 minutes). No new email means the trouble is gone.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## How you know it worked

- The next run of that watch sends nothing.
- Seen 3 September 2026: two invoice refusals on one probe job raised one dead-letter email and one human-review email at 05:30 UTC, and the 05:45 run repeated neither.
- Seen 4 September 2026, each provoked once on purpose and each landing once: five wake-up posts sent to a route that does not exist ("6 wake-up posts did not arrive", the sixth was a real one), three timed jobs made to divide by zero ("3 scheduled jobs failing"), a queued action nobody owns ("1 queued action no drain owns"), the payroll threshold widened to 200 days ("the payroll calendar runs out in 111 days"), a probe payment deleted in Zoho ("a CRM delete of a payment was ignored", 4 minutes after the delete), and the sync worker's wake-up paused for 23 minutes with one message waiting ("1 queue stalled, oldest 21m", on the 13:30 UTC run). The 13:15 run found the same wake-up trouble and sent nothing.
- A watch keyed on the newest failure sends again when a new failure joins the old ones: the wake-failure email went out a second time nine minutes later as "7 wake-up posts did not arrive" when one more real post timed out. That is by design; read the newest line, not the count.
- The same six were provoked again at 17:44 UTC the same day and every one landed on the 17:45 wake, within 30 seconds: "1 queued action no drain owns", "5 wake-up posts did not arrive", "3 scheduled jobs failing", "43 events await a person" (one flag raised on purpose), "the reconciliation sweep has not run in 4 hours" (threshold tightened to 1 hour) and "the payroll calendar runs out in 111 days" (threshold widened to 150 days). The next wake, at 17:50, added "1 property has no street-view image" for a probe property left a day without one, and "42 events await a person" once the probe flag was closed. At 17:51 a paid probe job cancelled in Zoho brought "INV008348 was PAID and its work order is cancelled". Every probe was removed afterwards and no queue was left holding anything.
- The wake-failure count climbs by one on every alert run, because the alert function's own wake takes longer than the 5 seconds the poster waits (<Linear issue="COM-202" />). "8 wake-up posts did not arrive" after five probe posts is that, not three more lost wakes.

## If the same alert keeps coming

The count in the subject is changing, so something new is failing each time. Look at the newest record in the body, not the oldest.
