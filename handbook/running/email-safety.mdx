Every email the engine sends passes one gate before it leaves. The gate has a short list of addresses that may receive mail. Anything else is not sent to the customer: it is sent to the operator instead, with the intended address written into the subject.

## The three outcomes

| The address is | What happens | How it looks |
|---|---|---|
| On the allowed list (the operator's own address today) | delivered as written | normal subject |
| At the test mail domain used for probe records | delivered as written | normal subject |
| Anything else, including every real customer | delivered to the operator | subject starts with **[TEST → the address it was meant for]** |

There is no fourth outcome. A mistake in a record, a typo, an old customer email: none of them can reach a person outside the company.

## A redirected copy is not the customer's email

The engine keeps a ledger of every email it sends, so nothing goes out twice. A copy the gate redirected to the operator is written in that ledger as redirected, not as sent: the customer has not had it, and the send is still owed. What happens next depends on the kind of email.

- **Reminders** (the day-before appointment reminder, the overdue-invoice reminder) are found again by their daily sweep while the redirected copy stands. The sweep sends again under a fresh ledger entry, and the operator gets another TEST copy, once a day for the invoice reminder and once for the appointment reminder, until the gate lets the real one through.
- **Everything else** (a booking confirmation, an invoice, a receipt, a quote, an alert) is sent once. A redirected copy counts as done for these, and running the same send again sends nothing.

Watched 5 September 2026 on a probe customer with a made-up address: the appointment reminder went out at 00:05:48 UTC and its ledger row read redirected. The sweep run again three minutes later picked the appointment up, marked the first row superseded, and sent a second copy under a new entry at 00:08:48, half a second end to end; both copies arrived in the operator's inbox with the TEST prefix. The probe invoice emailed earlier that evening was asked to send again at 00:12:29 and declined, because it already carried its sent time. Before that day a redirected reminder was written as sent, so the customer would never have received the real one (<Linear issue="COM-198" />).

## What was seen

On 3 September 2026 every email in the walk went through this gate: booking confirmations, the invoice, the receipt, the quote, the technician's app invite. The ones addressed to a probe address at the test domain arrived there. One addressed to a made-up customer address arrived in the operator's inbox with the TEST prefix, and the engine logged it as delivered to the operator instead.

## Turning it on for real

Widening the allowed list is a decision, not a setting. It lives in the engine's code, and changing it is a code change that goes through review, so nobody can flip it in a hurry. When the day comes, the list is replaced by the rule "any address", and the TEST prefix stops appearing.
