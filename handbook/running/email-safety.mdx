Every email the engine sends passes one gate before it leaves. The gate has a short list of addresses that may receive mail. Anything else is not sent to the customer: it is sent to the operator instead, with the intended address written into the subject.

## The three outcomes

| The address is | What happens | How it looks |
|---|---|---|
| On the allowed list (the operator's own address today) | delivered as written | normal subject |
| At the test mail domain used for probe records | delivered as written | normal subject |
| Anything else, including every real customer | delivered to the operator | subject starts with **[TEST → the address it was meant for]** |

There is no fourth outcome. A mistake in a record, a typo, an old customer email: none of them can reach a person outside the company.

## What was seen

On 3 September 2026 every email in the walk went through this gate: booking confirmations, the invoice, the receipt, the quote, the technician's app invite. The ones addressed to a probe address at the test domain arrived there. One addressed to a made-up customer address arrived in the operator's inbox with the TEST prefix, and the engine logged it as delivered to the operator instead.

## Turning it on for real

Widening the allowed list is a decision, not a setting. It lives in the engine's code, and changing it is a code change that goes through review, so nobody can flip it in a hurry. When the day comes, the list is replaced by the rule "any address", and the TEST prefix stops appearing.
